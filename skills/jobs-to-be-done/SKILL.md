# Jobs to be done

People don't want the product, they want the progress. Frame the need as the job, and the product becomes one way to get it done.

## Steps

1. **Find the moment of switching** in the research: when someone started looking for a new way, what triggered it, what they compared, and what they chose.
2. **Write the main job statement:** "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome]." For example: "When my insurance renewal goes up, I want to see quickly whether I'm overpaying, so I can switch without risking worse cover."
3. **Map the four forces:**
   - Push of the current situation (what's wrong now)
   - Pull of the new solution (what they hope for)
   - Anxiety about the new (what could go wrong)
   - Habit of the present (why staying is easy)
4. **List related jobs:** functional (get it done), emotional (feel in control), social (look responsible).
5. **Desired outcomes:** what "done well" means, measurable where possible ("know my real price in under two minutes").

## Output

The main job statement, the four forces as a two by two list, related jobs, and the desired outcomes, each linked to the research it came from.

## Don't

- Don't write jobs that name your product or a feature. Don't skip anxiety and habit: they're why people don't switch.
