# Card sorting and tree testing

Card sorting shows how people group things. Tree testing shows whether they can find things in a structure. Use the first to build a navigation, the second to check it.

## Card sort

1. **Cards:** 30 to 60 real content items, written in plain language, no category hints in the names.
2. **Type:** open (people make and name groups) to discover, closed (fixed groups) to check a proposal, hybrid for both.
3. **Participants:** 15 to 30 for unmoderated sorts.
4. **Analysis:** which cards are grouped together most often (a similarity matrix), the group names people use, and the cards that land everywhere (they need a clearer label or a second home).

## Tree test

1. **Tree:** the proposed navigation as text only, no design.
2. **Tasks:** 8 to 10 "Where would you find..." tasks for important or tricky items, worded without the label's words.
3. **Participants:** 30 to 50.
4. **Analysis per task:** success rate, directness (got there without backtracking), first click, and where people went wrong.

## Output

For a card sort: recommended groups and labels with the evidence. For a tree test: per task results, the problem areas, and specific label or structure changes, then what to re test.

## Don't

- Don't use your org chart as the tree. Don't test labels with the words that appear in the task.
