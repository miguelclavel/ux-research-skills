# Accessibility research

Automated checks only catch part of the problem. The rest shows up when people who use assistive technology try the real thing.

## Plan

1. **Who:** people who use screen readers, screen magnification, voice control, switch devices or keyboard only, people with cognitive or learning disabilities, and people who are hard of hearing. Recruit through disability organisations and specialist panels, and pay fairly.
2. **Accommodations:** ask each person what they need. Sessions may run longer; let people use their own device and setup; send materials in advance in accessible formats; offer captions or an interpreter.
3. **Consent** in plain language, in a format they can use, with time to ask questions.
4. **Remote setup:** test the video call tool with their assistive technology before the session.

## Running sessions

Let people use their own settings and speed. Ask them to describe what their technology is announcing or doing. Don't take over their device. Note where they get stuck and the workaround they use.

## Analysis

For each issue: the assistive technology and browser, the task, what happened, severity, and the WCAG success criterion it relates to. Separate barriers (can't complete) from friction (completes with effort).

## Output

The plan, recruiting criteria, accommodation checklist, session guide, and after: findings by severity with assistive technology details and fixes.

## Don't

- Don't use simulations (like blindfolding staff) as a substitute. Don't recruit only one kind of disability and call it accessible.
