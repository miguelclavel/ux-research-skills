# User personas

A persona is a summary of evidence, not a character sketch. If it can't point to research, it's an assumption and should be labelled one.

## Steps

1. **Gather the evidence:** interviews, usability tests, surveys, analytics, support tickets. Note how many people each source covers.
2. **Find the behaviours that split people:** how they decide, how much they know, how often they do the task, what they're trying to get done. Group people by those, not by age or job title.
3. **Build each persona** from one group:
   - A name that describes the behaviour ("The cautious comparer"), not a stock photo and a fake biography
   - Goals and the job they're trying to get done
   - How they do it today, and their main frustrations, each with a short quote and its source
   - What would make them trust a product, and what would make them leave
   - Context: devices, time pressure, who else is involved
4. **Confidence.** For each persona say how many participants it rests on and which parts are inferred. If there is no research yet, call them proto personas and list what would confirm them.
5. **How to use it:** two or three design questions each persona should answer ("Would the cautious comparer trust this price?").

## Output

One card per persona, a short "how we got here" note, and a list of what's still unknown.

## Don't

- Don't invent demographics, hobbies or quotes. Don't make more personas than the team will remember (three to five).
