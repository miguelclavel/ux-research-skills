# Research readout

Nobody acts on a 40 page report. Lead with what changes, and make every claim easy to check.

## Structure

1. **The headline:** the one thing the team should do differently, in a sentence.
2. **What we did:** method, who took part (counts and segments), when. Two lines.
3. **Insights,** three to five, each as: the insight in one sentence ("People don't trust a price until they see which insurer is behind it"), the evidence (how many participants, two quotes with ids, supporting data), what it means for the product, and a confidence level (strong, moderate, early signal).
4. **Recommendations,** each tied to an insight, with an owner and what would show it worked.
5. **What we still don't know,** and the next research step.
6. **Appendix:** method details, the guide, and the raw findings for anyone who wants them.

## Make it travel

A one page summary someone can forward, and a short clip or quote per insight. Remove names; use participant ids.

## Don't

- Don't present findings without evidence counts. Don't bury the decision on page ten. Don't overstate: eight interviews show patterns, not percentages.
