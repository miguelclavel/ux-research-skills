# Research plan

Research exists to change a decision. Start from the decision, not the method.

## Steps

1. **The decision.** What will the team decide differently depending on what we learn? If nothing, push back: it may not need research.
2. **What we already know.** Analytics, past research, support tickets, sales notes. Don't research what's already answered.
3. **Research questions.** Three to five questions about people's behaviour and context, not about our product ideas. "How do people choose an insurance plan today?" not "Would people like our new quote tool?"
4. **Pick the method from the question:**
   - Why and how people do things today: interviews, contextual inquiry, diary study
   - Whether people can use something: usability testing
   - How many people do or think something: survey, analytics
   - How people group or find information: card sorting, tree testing
   - Which version performs better: A/B test
5. **Participants.** Who, how many (five to eight per segment for interviews and usability tests is usually enough to see patterns), how to recruit, incentives, and accessibility needs.
6. **Ethics and privacy.** Consent, what's recorded, who sees it, how long it's kept, and how names are removed from notes.
7. **Timeline and owners**, and the readout: when, to whom, in what form.

## Output

A one page plan: decision, what we know, research questions, method and why, participants, ethics, timeline, deliverable.

## Don't

- Don't pick the method first. Don't ask users to design the solution. Don't promise statistical certainty from eight interviews.
