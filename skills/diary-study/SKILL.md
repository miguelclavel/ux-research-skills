# Diary study

Some things only show up over time: a renewal decision, a new habit, a slow frustration. A diary study captures them as they happen.

## Plan

1. **Why a diary study:** the behaviour spreads over days or weeks, or happens at moments you can't be present for.
2. **Length and cadence:** usually one to four weeks; daily prompts for habits, event based prompts ("each time you check your balance") for occasional moments.
3. **Participants:** eight to fifteen, over recruited, because people drop out. Start with a short onboarding call.
4. **Tool:** something they already use on their phone (a form, a messaging app, a study platform) so logging takes under five minutes.
5. **Prompts:** short, concrete, and about what just happened. "What were you trying to do? What happened? A photo or screenshot if you can." Vary them so people don't autopilot.
6. **Keep people engaged:** a short reply to entries, a mid study check in, and incentives split across the study.
7. **Privacy:** tell people what not to share (account numbers, other people's faces), and remove names from entries.

## Analysis

Read entries per person first (their story over time), then across people by theme. Close with a debrief interview on the moments that stood out.

## Output

The plan, the prompt schedule, the onboarding script, and after the study: themes over time, key moments, and quotes with participant ids and dates.
