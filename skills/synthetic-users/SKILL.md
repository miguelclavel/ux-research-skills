# Synthetic users

Asking an AI to role play users is fast and tempting, and it can't replace people: it tells you what's typical in its training data, not what your users do. Used carefully, it's good for one thing: getting sharper before you talk to real people.

## Good uses

- **Stress test assumptions:** "What would make a cautious comparer not trust this price?" Then check the answers with real users.
- **Sharpen a guide:** rehearse interview questions and spot leading or confusing ones.
- **Anticipate objections** and edge cases to cover in a test.
- **Widen the frame:** ask for perspectives the team hasn't considered (people using screen readers, people on slow connections, people new to the country), then recruit those people.

## How

1. **Ground each synthetic user in real research** when you have it (a persona and quotes), and say when you don't.
2. **Ask for reasoning, not verdicts:** "What would you look for first, and why?" rather than "Would you buy this?"
3. **Generate several distinct perspectives,** and ask each what would make them leave.
4. **Turn every output into a hypothesis** with a plan to test it: "Hypothesis: people won't trust a price without seeing the insurer. Test: usability task 2."

## Output

A list of hypotheses and better questions, each labelled "synthetic, not evidence", with how to validate it.

## Don't

- Don't count synthetic answers as participants, put them in research reports as findings, quote them as users, or use them to decide whether something ships.
