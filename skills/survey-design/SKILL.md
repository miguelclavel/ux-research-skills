# Survey design

Surveys count things. Use them when you need to know how many, not why.

## Steps

1. **Start from what you'll do with the answers.** Every question maps to a decision or an analysis. Cut the rest.
2. **Ask about behaviour and facts first,** opinions second. "How many times did you compare quotes last year?" before "How satisfied are you?"
3. **Write neutral questions:** one idea per question, no leading words, no double negatives, balanced scales with a neutral point and a "not applicable" when needed.
4. **Order:** easy and relevant first, sensitive and demographic last, demographics optional.
5. **Length:** under ten minutes. Long surveys get rushed answers.
6. **Sample:** say who it's going to, how they're reached, and who's missing (people who already left, people who don't open email). For estimating a percentage, a few hundred responses gives a usefully narrow range; explain the margin rather than overselling precision.
7. **Pilot** with five people and fix what confused them.
8. **Privacy:** collect only what you need, say how it's used, keep answers anonymous where possible.

## Output

The survey with question types and answer options, the reason for each question, the analysis plan, and the sampling notes.

## Don't

- Don't ask people to predict their future behaviour and treat it as a forecast. Don't make every question required.
