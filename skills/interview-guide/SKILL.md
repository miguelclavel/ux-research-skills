# Interview guide

Good interviews ask about the past, not the future. People are bad at predicting what they'll do and good at telling you what they did.

## Structure for a 30 to 45 minute session

1. **Intro (3 min).** Who you are, the purpose in plain words, consent to record, "there are no wrong answers, we're testing ideas, not you".
2. **Warm up (5 min).** Their context and role, not your product.
3. **The core (20 to 30 min).** Walk through the last time they actually did the task: "Tell me about the last time you..." then let the story run. Questions follow the order things happened.
4. **Workarounds and costs (5 min).** What they tried instead, what it cost in time, money or stress.
5. **Close (3 min).** "What should I have asked?", thanks, next steps for the incentive.

## Rules for every question

- Open ended, about real past behaviour, one idea at a time.
- No leading ("Wouldn't it be easier if..."), no yes or no, no hypotheticals ("Would you use...").
- After each core question, a probe: "What happened next?", "Why was that?", "How did you decide?", "Can you show me?"

## Output

The guide with timings, each question followed by its probe, and the three questions to keep if time runs short. Then a short list of what to listen for, tied to the research questions.

## Don't

- Don't pitch. Don't fill silences. Don't correct the participant.
