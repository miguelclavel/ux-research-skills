# Screener survey

A screener finds people who match the behaviour you're studying, while hiding which answers get them in.

## Steps

1. **Define who you need by behaviour,** not demographics: "compared car insurance quotes in the last six months", not "aged 25 to 40".
2. **Write disguised questions.** Multiple choice where the qualifying answer sits among plausible others, so people can't guess their way in.
3. **Screen out professionals and repeat testers:** people who work in UX, research, marketing or the industry being studied, and anyone who joined a study in the last few months.
4. **Add one open text question** to check people can express themselves ("Tell us about the last time you..."), which also catches bots.
5. **Set quotas** for the mix you need (devices, experience levels, assistive technology users) and over recruit by about 20% for no shows.
6. **Ask about accessibility needs** so you can accommodate, not exclude.
7. **Keep it short:** under ten questions. Collect only the personal data you need, and say how it's used.

## Output

The screener with each question, its answer options, and which answers qualify, disqualify or need review, plus the quota table.

## Don't

- Don't ask "Are you interested in X?" (everyone is). Don't reveal the product or the company if it biases who applies.
